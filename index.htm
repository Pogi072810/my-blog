Is it fair?

Higher income.
A bigger share.

We think progressive income tax is fair in principle: people with more taxable income can contribute more.

At ₱300,000, personal income tax takes 2.50%. At ₱1,000,000, it takes 15.25%.

But fairness also depends on living costs, family needs, other taxes, and how public money is spent.

A FEW USEFUL NOTES

What income is tax-exempt?+
  • 13th-month pay and other qualifying benefits: exempt up to a combined ₱90,000 a year. The excess becomes taxable.
  • Small employee benefits: exempt within BIR limits. Mandatory employee SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG contributions are excluded from taxable compensation.
  • Dependents: TRAIN removed separate personal and dependent exemptions. The ₱250,000 zero-tax threshold applies regardless of marital status or dependents.
Business deductions & other taxes +

Business deductions

Eligible self-employed people can choose the 8% option or graduated rates on net taxable income. With graduated rates, use itemized allowable expenses or OSD: 40% of gross sales/receipts for individuals. For eligible corporations, OSD is 40% of gross income. Percentage tax may also apply.

Other common taxes

For resident individuals: Philippine peso bank interest commonly has 20% final tax; domestic corporate dividends, 10%. Donor’s tax is 6% on total net gifts above ₱250,000 per year. Estate tax is 6% of the net taxable estate after deductions.

Local taxes

Local business and real property taxes depend on local ordinances and applicable law.
